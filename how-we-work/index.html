How we work

From the market to the steam header.

Most electrification studies stop at equipment. The economics live in the power contract and in knowing which hours to run. We cover the equipment, the contract and the market analysis behind both.

What we do

Four kinds of work, one decision.

Electrification

Flexible electric heat

Steam and hot water that runs on electricity when power is cheap and on gas when it isn't, sized and dispatched from the price history your sites actually face.

  • Electric and dual-fuel boilers, steam-generating heat pumps, electric-thermal storage
  • Sizing against hour-by-hour price history, not averages
  • Dispatch logic: when to switch, and for how long

Contracting

Large load power contracting

A flexible load is only worth what its contract lets it capture. We structure supply so the cheap hours actually reach your bill.

  • Real-time pricing tariffs and real-time index supply
  • Regulated utility tariffs, riders, and finding ultra-low off-peak rates
  • Avoiding capacity, transmission and demand charges by staying off the peaks
  • Interconnection and large load process support
  • Negotiating special contracts with suppliers and utilities

Procurement

PPAs and renewable supply

Virtual and physical PPAs, on-site generation and renewable attributes that line up with your scope 2 goals and your load shape.

  • VPPA strategy, RFPs, term sheet and settlement analysis
  • Basis and shape risk across hubs and nodes
  • Matching a flexible load to a PPA's hourly shape

Analysis

Wholesale market analysis

How prices form, when generators turn off, and what that means for a site's cost. The same work behind ERCOT Bids, applied to your market and your load.

  • Price duration and gas parity across ERCOT, SPP, MISO, or any hub or node
  • Bid stack and generator behavior at low and negative prices
  • Decarbonization screening across multi-site portfolios

How a project runs

A defined process for a complicated decision.

  1. Screen

    A free first pass from your utility bills. Within about a week you get your sites ranked by potential, with the technology that fits each one: electric boiler, heat pump or thermal storage.

  2. Design

    Pick the technology and size it against real price history, with gas kept as the backup.

  3. Contract

    Choose the supply structure, tariff and any PPA so the savings show up on the bill.

  4. Operate

    We set the switching rules with your controls team, from a simple price threshold to day-ahead schedules, then run or support dispatch and track savings against the plan. Your operators keep the plant, and the gas boilers are always there.

For utilities and renewable owners

A floor under the price of renewable power.

More hours clear at $0/MWh or below every year, and someone holds the gap between zero and that $55 PPA on your books. Flexible industrial heat can be the floor: a megawatt-hour that displaces gas from a boiler is worth about $15 in every hour it runs.

Approach 1

Heat as a service, sold as a hedge

A transmission-connected industrial site buys electric heat at $15/MWh. That contract guarantees $15 whenever the market clears below it, a floor you can sell to offtakers or hold against your own PPAs.

Approach 2

A non-firm, dispatchable tariff

Cheap, interruptible power in the hours you have surplus energy and headroom on the wires. Buy at wholesale, sell to flexible load at a floor, and put the margin toward the next renewable contract.

Either way: more kilowatt-hours sold, revenue from power that would otherwise be curtailed, and progress on clean heat and gas decarbonization targets. We are looking for pilots.

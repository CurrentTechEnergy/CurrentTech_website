Intermittent electrification

Keep your gas boilers. Add electric beside them.

An electric boiler or heat pump runs in parallel with the existing gas system. It takes power only in cheap, high-renewable hours, often power that would otherwise be curtailed, and the gas boilers carry the rest. That costs far less than going all-electric 24/7. In those hours the marginal generator is usually wind or solar, so the heat it displaces from gas is close to carbon-free.

Why it works for you

  • Keep steam, so nothing downstream changes
  • Low capex, with operating cost at or below gas in the hours it runs
  • Less exposure to energy price spikes, because gas covers them
  • Electricity has a known path to zero carbon through RECs and PPAs. Gas doesn't.
  • Ready for hourly carbon accounting: the GHG Protocol's Scope 2 update may require matching power to the hour it was used, and flexible loads are easier to match
  • Or buy the steam: CurrentTech can own and run the electric heat and sell you steam at or below your gas cost, with no capital outlay

Why it works for the grid

  • Soaks up wind and solar that would otherwise be curtailed
  • Puts a floor under prices in the hours renewables would otherwise earn nothing, which keeps new renewables getting built
  • Runs off-peak by design, so it adds no load when the grid is tight

Sites in wind- and solar-heavy regions, such as ERCOT, SPP and MISO, usually have the most cheap hours. But send us every site. The screening ranks them.

Intermittent electrification

Keep your gas boilers. Add electric beside them.

An electric boiler or heat pump runs in parallel with the existing gas system. It takes power only in cheap, high-renewable hours, often power that would otherwise be curtailed, and the gas boilers carry the rest. That costs far less than going all-electric 24/7, and it can emit less too.

Why it works for you

  • Keep steam, so nothing downstream changes
  • Low capex, with operating costs at or below gas
  • Less exposure to energy price spikes, because gas covers them
  • Electricity has a known path to zero carbon through RECs and PPAs. Gas doesn't.

Why it works for the grid

  • As renewables grow, market revenue in sunny and windy hours heads toward $0/MWh. Flexible load puts a floor under it.
  • Soaks up curtailed wind and solar instead of wasting it
  • Ready for hourly matching: the GHG Protocol's Scope 2 update may require it, and flexible loads are easier to match

What a site needs: hourly electricity pricing, a grid with plenty of renewables, and reasonable distribution costs, with no non-coincident demand charges that punish running off-peak.

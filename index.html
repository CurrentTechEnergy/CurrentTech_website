Heat with power when it's cheaper than gas.

As grids add wind and solar, wholesale power gets cheap enough to make heat for less than gas, often for more than a thousand hours a year. CurrentTech helps campuses, hospitals and industrial sites capture those hours, in whatever market they sit: flexible electric heat, the power contracts behind it, and the market analysis that ties them together.

Cheap hours aren't rare. They're a resource.

Texas is the clearest case, and we track it daily: every hour of 2025, real-time ERCOT prices against what it cost to make the same heat with gas that day. What we do

From the price signal to the steam header.

Most electrification studies stop at equipment. The economics live in the power contract and in knowing which hours to run. We work across all three.

Electrification

Flexible electric heat

Steam and hot water that runs on electricity when power is cheap and on gas when it isn't. Hybrid systems keep the existing boilers and distribution, so you aren't paying for a campus-wide hot water conversion.

  • Electric boilers, industrial heat pumps, electric-thermal storage
  • Sizing against hour-by-hour price history, not averages
  • Dispatch logic: when to switch, and for how long

Contracting

Large load power contracting

A flexible load is only worth what its contract lets it capture. We structure supply so the cheap hours actually reach your bill.

  • Real-time index, block and hybrid supply structures in competitive markets
  • Regulated utility tariffs, riders, demand charges and transmission cost exposure
  • Interconnection and large load process support
  • Negotiating special contracts with suppliers and utilities

Procurement

PPAs and renewable supply

Virtual and physical PPAs, on-site generation and renewable attributes that line up with your scope 2 goals and your load shape.

  • VPPA strategy, RFPs, term sheet and settlement analysis
  • Basis and shape risk across hubs and nodes
  • Experience on four wind deals totaling hundreds of MW in ERCOT, SPP and AESO

Analysis

Wholesale market analysis

How prices form, when generators turn off, and what that means for a site's cost. The same work behind ERCOT Bids, applied to your market and your load.

  • Price duration and gas parity across ERCOT, SPP, MISO, or any hub or node
  • Bid stack and generator behavior at low and negative prices
  • Decarbonization screening across multi-site portfolios

MISO · Wisconsin

ATC transmission peak identification

Transmission costs in American Transmission Company's footprint are set by load during a handful of system peak hours. This project forecasts which hours will set those peaks, so large customers know when cutting or shifting load lowers what they pay for transmission all year.

  • Peak-hour forecasting from load, weather and market data
  • Alerts timed so facilities can act ahead of the peak

How a project runs

A defined process for a complicated decision.

  1. Screen

    Rank your sites by power prices, heating load, interconnection and space. Cut energy waste first.

  2. Design

    Pick the technology and size it against real price history, with gas kept as the backup.

  3. Contract

    Choose the supply structure, tariff and any PPA so the savings show up on the bill.

  4. Operate

    Set the switching rules and track actual savings against the plan.
